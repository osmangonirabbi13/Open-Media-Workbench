# ADR-0004: Timeline Timebase and Edit Decision List (EDL)

**Status:** PROPOSED  
**Date:** 2026-10-13  
**Authors:** OpenTake Core Architecture Team  
**Deciders:** Lead Systems Architect, Media Pipeline Lead  
**Target Release:** v0.1.0 Beta  

---

## 1. Context & Problem Statement
Non-destructive editing requires tracking time boundaries across dozens or hundreds of cuts. Using floating-point seconds causes cumulative rounding errors, leading to A/V desynchronization and frame tearing. We must establish an exact, deterministic timebase model.

## 2. Decision Drivers
- Zero cumulative rounding drift over multi-hour recordings and hundreds of cuts.
- Compatibility with fractional film and NTSC framerates (23.976, 24, 25, 29.97, 59.94 fps).
- Audio sample-boundary alignment (44.1 kHz, 48 kHz).
- Clean mathematical interval operations (split, trim, merge, delete, restore).

## 3. Options Considered
- **Option 1 (Signed 64-bit Integer Microseconds `i64` with Rational Snapping):** All timeline coordinates stored in integer microseconds ($10^{-6}$ s); cuts snap to rational frame/audio boundaries.
- **Option 2 (Pure Rational Math Everywhere `num_rational::Rational64`):** Storing all timestamps as exact integer fractions `num/den`.
- **Option 3 (Floating-Point Seconds `f64`):** Storing timestamps as standard double-precision floats.

## 4. Proposed Decision
Adopt **Option 1 (Signed 64-bit Integer Microseconds `i64` with Rational Boundary Snapping)**.

## 5. Consequences & Tradeoffs
- **Positive:** Maximum single-frame quantization error is $< 0.5\,\mu\text{s}$ (less than $1/30{,}000\text{th}$ of a video frame). Microsecond arithmetic is lightning-fast and serializes cleanly to JSON without custom rational serializers.
- **Negative:** Requires explicit snapping math when translating microsecond coordinates to discrete video frame indices and audio sample boundaries.
- **Tradeoff Accepted:** Snapping logic is isolated in pure Rust functions and tested via unit test suites.

## 6. Risk Assessment & Alternatives Rejected
- Option 3 was rejected because floating-point drift creates progressive A/V desync.
- Option 2 was rejected as overly complex for UI state serialization and IPC transport.
